# Presentation script (5-minute lightning round)

Format, per the syllabus: the final presentation is not a live class
session. It is a group of 4 to 5 students who find a time to meet,
record the meeting on Zoom, present in lightning format, give each
other feedback, and share the recording with Prof. Tanks. Same
structure as the midterm. Schedule that call before Sunday, since the
recording link is part of the deliverable.

Target 4:45 spoken. Roughly 620 words. Share your screen and drive the
live site. No slides.

## 0:00 to 0:40, the hook

Open on the start page. Title: Keep the why. Then the line: "After
every trade, write why you did it."

Say this, slowly: I have been trying to get models to trade. You can
copy a strategy and test it on old prices. I have done that. What the
test never sees is why anyone clicked buy or sell. People get scared.
Prices jump. The file a model trains on usually just says the number
moved.

## 0:40 to 1:20, the argument

Train on prices alone and the model gets the leftover of a panic. Write
why after every trade and the model gets the trick with a name on it:
fear, hope, a rumor. That is the log I want in the carousel.

I am wiring a few models together. They trade, they write why, they
read each other. This archive is twelve cases where the why was public
and the surviving file is just the number.

Trouillot: silences enter at the moment a fact is made. A stored price
is that silence. Kahneman and Tversky: a loss feels about twice as hard
as an equal win. That feeling rarely makes it into the training file.
Caswell, reading Arendt: thoughtlessness in documentation is the harm.
The leftover price file is that thoughtlessness at scale.

## 1:20 to 2:10, the demo. This is the moment, do not rush it.

Stay on the start page. Read the GameStop paragraph out loud. Press
"Keep the numbers." Let the room watch the words leave until leftover
prices take their place. Then "Keep the why": the same paragraph,
labeled as feelings a model can train on. Then "See them trade": three
lines starting at $100. Numbers only finish at $71. A person in the
fear finishes at $55. The carousel, reading why, finishes at $134.
Say: this is a sketch of the claim, not a live track record.

## 2:10 to 2:50, the metadata intervention

Open any item record. Point at the two dates. Date of event is when the
market moved. Date created is when someone spoke. Most archives collapse
those. I made the gap a field, because a panic and a report written 90
days later belong in different drawers. Also point at
reasoningPresence: stated versus absent. Absent is the training file.
Stated is what the carousel needs.

## 2:50 to 3:20, the audiences

Public: anyone asked to trust a computer with money, and anyone
building the computer. One question: was the why saved?

Scholarly: digital humanities knows how to ask what a record threw
away, and has almost never asked that of market data. Machine learning
knows how to train on a file, and almost never asks what the file
deleted. This is a datasheet for the missing why.

## 3:20 to 3:50, the thing I am proud of, and the thing I am implicated in

Item eleven is my own five-year test. No reasons saved. I put it in
under my name because the carousel I am building would learn nothing
from that file. Item twelve is a language model explaining March 2020
six years late. Fluent, and not a reason log.

## 3:50 to 4:20, the choices I would defend

I link out and mirror nothing. Some of these records are people writing
at 2am about their own money. I built this outside Omeka because "is
the why in the document" is not a default Dublin Core field. The Omeka
CSV still ships.

## 4:20 to 4:45, the call to action

After every trade, write why. Train on that. If you might put money
in, ask whether the why was logged. A no still lets you take the bet.
You would be betting on a model whose training data had no fear in it.

Close on the hero line again.
